# Outline — Genre in Literature

## Working Title
Genre in Literature: Forms, Conventions, Hybrids, and Reader Expectations

## Research Notes / Grounding
- Genre means a category or kind of literature, but it is not merely a shelf label; it is a system of conventions shared by writers, readers, publishers, and cultures.
- Classical genres included epic, lyric, drama, tragedy, and comedy; modern genres include novel, short story, romance, detective fiction, science fiction, fantasy, horror, realism, satire, dystopia, and many hybrids.
- Genre creates expectations about plot, character, setting, tone, style, ending, and reader response.
- Genres are historically changing. What counts as a novel, tragedy, romance, or realism varies across periods and cultures.
- Genre hybridity is common: tragicomedy, historical romance, comic epic, literary fantasy, dystopian satire, gothic realism.
- Strong genre analysis should ask how a work follows, revises, mixes, or resists conventions.

## Purpose
Create a long-form guide explaining genre as a living literary system of form, convention, and expectation.

## Audience
General literature readers, students, teachers, and serious beginners. Analytical and accessible.

## Structure
1. Introduction — genre is a reading contract, not just a label.
2. What Is Genre? — definition and etymological sense of kind/type.
3. Why Genre Matters — expectation, interpretation, marketing, literary tradition.
4. Genre vs Form vs Mode — clarify terms.
5. Major Literary Genres — poetry, drama, fiction, nonfiction; tragedy/comedy/epic/lyric/novel/short story.
6. Genre Conventions — plot patterns, character roles, settings, tone, endings, themes.
7. Genre and Reader Expectations — promise, surprise, satisfaction, violation.
8. Genre Hybrids — mixing forms and conventions.
9. Genre and History — genres change with culture and literary periods.
10. Literary vs Popular Genre — problem with false hierarchy.
11. Examples — detective fiction, gothic novel, tragedy, comedy, dystopia, bildungsroman.
12. Common Mistakes.
13. How to Analyze Genre.
14. FAQ.
15. Conclusion.
