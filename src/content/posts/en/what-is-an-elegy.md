## Quick Answer

An elegy is a poem of mourning, usually for someone who has died, but it may also reflect on loss.

## Simple Explanation

An elegy is literature’s quiet way of grieving. It moves from sorrow toward reflection or acceptance.

## Easy Examples

Thomas Gray’s Elegy Written in a Country Churchyard and Milton’s Lycidas are famous examples.

A good exam example should be short, accurate, and directly connected to the definition. Do not write a famous name unless you can explain why it fits.

## Exam Angle

Features: lament, praise, consolation, serious tone, meditation on death.

## Common Mistakes and Confusions

Do not confuse elegy with eulogy: a eulogy is a speech of praise, usually at a funeral.

Also avoid mixing the term with nearby terms. When two terms look similar, compare their function: what does each term do inside a poem, play, or story?
