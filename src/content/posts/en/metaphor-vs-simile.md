## Quick Answer

A simile compares two things using “like” or “as”; a metaphor says one thing is another.

## Simple Explanation

Simile is a visible comparison. Metaphor is a stronger identity-style comparison.

## Easy Examples

Simile: “Her smile is like sunshine.” Metaphor: “Her smile is sunshine.”

A good exam example should be short, accurate, and directly connected to the definition. Do not write a famous name unless you can explain why it fits.

## Exam Angle

Difference questions are frequent. Mention comparison, markers, directness, and effect.

For competitive exams, prepare a 3-line answer:

1. definition,
2. two features,
3. one example.

## Common Mistakes and Confusions

Do not call every comparison a metaphor; check for “like” or “as”.

Also avoid mixing the term with nearby terms. When two terms look similar, compare their function: what does each term do inside a poem, play, or story?
