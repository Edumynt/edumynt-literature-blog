## Quick Answer

Imagery is language that creates sense pictures—sight, sound, smell, taste, touch, or movement.

## Simple Explanation

Imagery helps readers “see” and “feel” the text in the mind.

## Easy Examples

“Golden daffodils” is visual imagery; “whispering wind” can be auditory imagery.

A good exam example should be short, accurate, and directly connected to the definition. Do not write a famous name unless you can explain why it fits.

## Exam Angle

Mention visual, auditory, olfactory, gustatory, tactile, and kinesthetic imagery.

For competitive exams, prepare a 3-line answer:

1. definition,
2. two features,
3. one example.

## Common Mistakes and Confusions

Do not limit imagery to only pictures; it includes all senses.

Also avoid mixing the term with nearby terms. When two terms look similar, compare their function: what does each term do inside a poem, play, or story?
