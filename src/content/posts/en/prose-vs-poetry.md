## Quick Answer

Prose is ordinary sentence-based writing; poetry is compressed, musical, and arranged in lines, rhythm, or images.

## Simple Explanation

Prose walks like normal speech. Poetry dances: it uses sound, rhythm, imagery, and fewer words to create a stronger effect.

If you are new to literature, connect this topic with [What Is Literature? Meaning, Scope, and Importance](/posts/what-is-literature/), [What Is Poetry? Meaning, Elements, and Types](/posts/what-is-poetry/). These basics make the rest of literary study much easier.

## Easy Examples

A novel is usually prose. A sonnet, ode, lyric, or elegy is poetry.

A good exam example should be short, accurate, and directly connected to the definition. Do not write a famous name unless you can explain why it fits.

## Exam Angle

Difference questions are common: compare form, language, rhythm, line breaks, and purpose.

For competitive exams, prepare a 3-line answer:

1. definition,
2. two features,
3. one example.

## Common Mistakes and Confusions

Do not confuse prose with “easy” and poetry with “difficult”. Both can be simple or complex.

Also avoid mixing the term with nearby terms. When two terms look similar, compare their function: what does each term do inside a poem, play, or story?
